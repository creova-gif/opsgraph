1. YC framing
1.1 The story in one paragraph
Startups build products first and reconstruct their internal operating infrastructure later—usually when an enterprise customer, investor, auditor, or hiring surge forces them to. OpsGraph connects to the workspace they already use, understands the company’s stage and context, identifies what operational infrastructure should exist, creates the approved missing structure and artifacts, and continuously monitors drift. We are starting with Google Workspace and B2B startups because the problem is acute and measurable there; the long-term product is a cross-platform operating graph for companies.
1.2 Why now
AI makes it economically possible to reason over unstructured company documents and produce context-specific drafts, mappings and explanations.
Workspaces and APIs make company state observable enough to build a maintenance loop rather than deliver a one-time consulting binder.
Agents are becoming common, which increases—not decreases—the need for a governed company model, permissions, provenance and shared operational state.
Enterprise buyers, investors and regulators continue to demand evidence, ownership and repeatable processes from startups earlier in their life.

3. Founder narrative
Use a factual founder story rather than credentials inflation. The strongest story is: while building and operating companies, you repeatedly had to create document systems, governance, policies, registers, engineering/research standards and compliance-ready structures. During a recent company operating-system build, the work required repeated judgment about taxonomy, numbering, provenance, missing claims, ownership and evidence. That revealed a repeatable product pattern: profile the company → model required state → compare with actual workspace → compile missing infrastructure → continuously maintain it.
Narrative rule
Do not say “I have many ventures, so I can do anything.” YC may read that as lack of focus. Say: “Repeated operating pain across my work led me to this problem; I am choosing this company and testing it now.”

6. Draft answer bank — adapt to exact YC form
What are you making?
We are building OpsGraph, software that turns an existing Google Workspace into a startup’s operating infrastructure. A founder connects their workspace and answers a structured onboarding. We inventory the current state, identify missing company infrastructure, generate and organize approved artifacts such as policies, registers, decision records and investor-readiness documents, and then monitor changes with an AI Company Guardian. The first users are B2B startups preparing for enterprise customers, fundraising or compliance.
Why did you pick this idea?
I kept encountering the same problem while building and operating companies: the product gets built, but the company infrastructure stays scattered across drives and people’s heads until a customer, investor or audit forces cleanup. In a recent operating-system build I had to make dozens of repeated decisions about taxonomy, ownership, document numbering, missing evidence and what could safely be asserted. The pattern was systematic enough to turn into software.
What do you understand that others do not?
The folder tree and templates are not the product. The real product is a required-state model of the company: given its stage, industry, jurisdiction, team and commitments, what should exist—and how does that compare with what exists today? Once that model exists, compliance, investor readiness, onboarding and document maintenance become different views over the same operating graph.
Who are your competitors?
Vanta, Drata and Secureframe automate compliance; Notion, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 are workspaces; Slite and similar products maintain knowledge; fractional COOs manually create operating systems. We are initially different in that we compile and maintain general company infrastructure inside the customer’s existing workspace, using a required-state model rather than being a new workspace or only a compliance product. This distinction still needs to be proven with customers.
How will you make money?
We are testing an implementation fee of roughly $750–$2,500 for early customers plus $99–$499/month for the maintenance agent. The long-term model is primarily recurring B2B SaaS, with higher-priced industry/framework packs and portfolio deployments for accelerators or investors if those channels validate.

8. Customer interview script
Tell me about the last time a customer, investor, employee or auditor asked you for company documents and you could not answer immediately.
What did you have to create or search for? Who did the work? How long did it take?
What is scattered or inconsistent today: policies, contracts, decisions, employee docs, security evidence, investor materials?
What happens if you do nothing for the next six months?
Have you paid a consultant, compliance tool, lawyer or operator to solve part of this? How much?
If we could install the missing operating infrastructure inside your current workspace in 48 hours, what would make that worth paying for now?

10. Application video preparation
Prepare a plain founder video with a direct structure: who you are; what you are building; the specific user/problem; what you have built or sold; why you are the person pursuing it; and one concrete traction fact. Follow the current application instructions exactly when recording because video requirements can change.
Founder-video rule
No agency-style promo reel, background music, buzzword montage or lengthy origin story. Clarity and founder credibility beat production value.


11. YC interview preparation
Interview answers should be short, numerical and responsive. Prepare deeper evidence, but answer the exact question first.
Likely challenge
Good response structure
Why isn’t this just Notion?
Not a workspace; we model required company state and compile/maintain it inside the existing workspace. Then show usage.
Why won’t Google/Microsoft build this?
They provide horizontal workspace primitives; our narrow company operating ontology and cross-platform readiness logic are the product. More importantly, show customers choosing you now.
Is this consulting?
Today we deliberately do manual work to learn. Show the percentage of delivery already repeated/automated and the path to high-margin software.
Why does a startup pay before it hurts?
We do not sell before pain. We target concrete triggers: enterprise deal, fundraise, security request, hiring. Show conversion by trigger.
How big can this be?
Start with readiness; expand into shared company operating graph across functions, stacks and agents.
Why you?
Firsthand operating pain + ability to build + speed of customer learning. Avoid a resume recital.
What if Vanta expands?
Compliance is one view; OpsGraph models general operating state. Still acknowledge that competitive encroachment is real.
How accurate/safe is the agent?
Explain provenance, approval gates, no autonomous compliance claims, audit trail and measured false positives.

13. Competitive answer discipline
Name competitors directly. Saying “we have no competitors” signals poor market understanding.
Do not attack incumbents; explain the different job or system boundary.
Do not claim a technical moat before you have one. Describe the data/ontology/workflow advantage you intend to compound.
Recognize that Notion and other workspace vendors already have agents; “we have an agent” is not differentiation.
Use customer evidence to prove the wedge rather than abstract feature comparisons.

17. What not to do before applying
Do not build Microsoft 365, Notion and Google connectors simultaneously.
Do not create 70 perfect templates before selling the first pilot.
Do not spend the month naming/rebranding the company.
Do not claim global/industry universality in the v1 pitch.
Do not fake traction using free friends-and-family “users.” Label pilots honestly.
Do not hide manual operations; explain how they teach you what to automate.
Do not claim compliance/certification outcomes the product cannot verify.
Do not write application answers like a pitch deck. Use short, factual, numerical language.
18. Founder focus decision
The biggest application risk is not competition; it is looking unfocused. If you apply with OpsGraph, your public/application narrative should make it clear that this is the company you are actively testing and building now. Other ventures can remain context or prior experience, but should not make the reviewer wonder which company receives your attention.
