VENTURE BUILD SYSTEM
Company OS — Venture Execution Blueprint
From concierge proof to AI-native company infrastructure platform
Founder working document • Version 1.0 • October 2026
Core thesis
Startups build products before they build the company infrastructure required to scale, sell to enterprises, raise capital, hire, and satisfy compliance demands. Company OS installs that missing infrastructure inside the workspace the company already uses, then continuously keeps it complete, current, owned, and auditable.

Recommended v1
Do not build “any industry, any stack, any geography” in v1. Start with 5–50 person B2B technology startups using Google Workspace, especially those approaching enterprise sales, fundraising, SOC 2 readiness, or rapid hiring. Build the architecture to expand later.

1. Venture definition
1.1 One-line definition
Company OS turns a startup’s existing workspace into a complete, AI-maintained operating system.
1.2 Customer promise
Keep the tools the company already uses; Company OS installs structure rather than forcing a workspace migration.
Know what operational artifacts should exist for the company’s stage, industry, geography, team, commercial situation, and target standards.
Generate and organize missing company infrastructure with explicit human approval and provenance.
Continuously detect missing, outdated, duplicated, ownerless, contradictory, or misplaced artifacts.
Produce readiness views for fundraising, enterprise sales, onboarding, security/compliance, audits, and internal scaling.
1.3 What Company OS is not
Not a generic document generator or template marketplace.
Not a replacement for Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Notion, or SharePoint.
Not a legal opinion, audit attestation, certification authority, or automated claim that a company is compliant.
Not another private chatbot. The long-term product is shared company infrastructure used by founders, operators, functional leaders, and agents.
Not an open-ended autonomous agent with unrestricted write access; risky actions remain approval-gated.

9. Guardian operating loop
Observe only authorized workspace changes using event/change mechanisms where possible.
Classify the changed artifact using deterministic rules first and model reasoning only when needed.
Compare metadata/content against canonical taxonomy, ownership, review cadence and dependency rules.
Create an exception rather than making a risky change when confidence or authorization is insufficient.
Auto-execute only reversible, low-risk operations explicitly approved during setup.
Produce a weekly “Company Health” brief: new risks, stale artifacts, missing evidence, unowned items, duplicates and recommended actions.
Record every agent action in an immutable activity trail suitable for audit and debugging.

16. Pilot delivery SOP
Pre-qualify: company has a real trigger and founder/operations owner will participate.
Collect payment before full build; discounts are allowed but free pilots do not validate willingness to pay.
Run structured onboarding; record every question the founder cannot answer.
Inventory workspace; do not modify originals during audit.
Create required-state map and explicit assumptions.
Present proposed taxonomy, artifact set and risky changes for approval.
Compile the OS, keeping missing factual content as clearly marked founder input—not invented assertions.
Run QA: duplicates, broken links, owners, numbering, dates, permissions and brand rendering.
Deliver before/after score and exception backlog.
Run Guardian weekly for four weeks and record every alert and false positive.
Conduct a 30-minute founder interview at day 1, day 14 and day 30.
Convert repeated judgment calls into rules, schemas, tests or product backlog items.

22. Product principles
Specific before broad: dominate one trigger, one ICP and one connector before expanding.
Evidence before assertion: the system must know what it observed, inferred, generated and still needs a human to confirm.
Propose before destructive action: trust is more important than autonomy.
Compile, do not dump templates: every artifact must exist for a reason derived from company context.
Operational graph over folder tree: workspaces are projections of a deeper company model.
Human judgment becomes product specification: every repeated decision from pilots should become a rule, test, schema or explicit escalation.
Sell outcomes: enterprise readiness, investor readiness, faster onboarding, audit preparedness—not cleanliness.
